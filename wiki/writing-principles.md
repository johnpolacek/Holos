# Writing Principles

The principles behind John's editing passes, collected as the edits are made. Each one names the edit it came from. Apply them site-wide. The [glossary](glossary.md) governs term names, and `.cursor/rules/holos-guardrails.mdc` governs what may be claimed.

## Principles

1. **Picture, not analogy.** Show the thing itself (a universe complete in every detail, with no one in it) rather than a comparison to something else (the buffet). Analogies drag in their own baggage and have to be unpacked. *Intro, 2026-09-30.*
2. **Make the claim.** Say the strong thing plainly ("Reality requires a witness") instead of a balanced summary ("needs both: not equations alone, and not experience alone"). *Intro, 2026-09-30.*
3. **Name the distinction early.** Give the reader the key word for the idea (*structure* vs *reality*) at the moment the idea appears. *Intro, 2026-09-30.*
4. **"Real" means lived.** Reserve *reality* and *real* for lived reality (R). What physics produces, lived or not, is *structure*. Unlit structure *exists* as pattern but is not called real. *Intro, 2026-09-30.*
5. **No insider jargon.** Cut phrases a newcomer has to decode ("nothing it is like to be"). Say it in ordinary words, or trust the next sentence to carry it. *Intro, 2026-09-30.*
6. **End on the hardest line.** Close a paragraph on its strongest sentence. Don't trail off into qualification. *Intro, 2026-09-30.*
7. **Cut the sentence the next one implies.** If a sentence only spells out what its neighbors already make clear ("Structure is realized only where it is experienced"), cut it. *Intro, 2026-09-30.*
8. **Experience changes nothing.** Avoid wording that makes experience act on the world ("to become realized"). Structure does not change when it is lived. It counts as reality where it is. *Intro, 2026-09-30.*
9. **Terse and plain.** Short sentences in ordinary words. No filler, no excess explanation, no unnecessary headings. *John, 2026-09-30.*
10. **Commas and periods.** Ordinary punctuation, mostly commas and periods. No em dashes, no semicolons. Go easy on colons. *John, 2026-09-30.*
11. **Two examples, not three.** Use the strongest two. A third, weaker one ("how probabilities evolve") dilutes the list and often brings jargon. *Intro, 2026-09-30.*
12. **Set up contrast with parallel sentences.** Repeat the frame so the turn lands ("It says how... It says nothing about..."). *Intro, 2026-09-30.*
13. **No throat-clearing lead-ins.** Open on what the thing is ("Holos is built on two ideas"), not on framing that makes ideas sound bolted on ("Holos adds two things to physics"). *Intro, 2026-09-30.*
14. **Don't overclaim in the frame.** "Built on" two ideas, not "comprised of" or "built from" them, since Holos is more than those two. *Intro, 2026-09-30.*
15. **One visual pattern for parallel terms.** Defined terms that come in a set go in small caps, one per line, behind a left rule. *Intro, 2026-09-30.*
16. **Links read "(see X)".** Name the destination in a short parenthetical, not descriptive link prose ("what kind of view this is", "its payoff and its price are stated"). *Claims box, 2026-09-30.*
17. **Keep the honesty markers.** Terse never means dropping what makes a claim credible: "it can fail", "should" on an untested prediction, the reason a fallback version exists. *Claims box, 2026-09-30.*
18. **No self-praise.** Cut words that grade our own ideas ("bold"). *Claims box, 2026-09-30.*
19. **Don't front-load.** One new idea per sentence, and not too many per paragraph. Introduce a term (aperture) before stacking the next one on it (Omega). *Consciousness, 2026-09-30.*
20. **Plain word at first contact.** Where a term is first defined for a newcomer, use the plainest word ("experienced"). Keep the defined term ("lived") for definitions and the formal pages. *Consciousness, 2026-09-30.*
21. **Examples that raise no side question.** Pick the example that makes one point cleanly (locked-in syndrome) over a more striking one that invites a detour (a dream: "is that real?"). *Consciousness, 2026-09-30.*
22. **Never define a term by itself.** "Integration, joined tightly enough to form one point of view" defines point of view by point of view. Say what is structurally different ("how fully a system's parts act as one"). *Consciousness, 2026-09-30.*
