## Part 2. Proposed figures

Format for each: **title** (anchor, line). What it shows. Stages. **Risk.** **Reuse.**

### Claims

**L1. One Fact, Two Additions** (`#minimal-core`, beside the "In brief" paragraph, L16)
- **Shows:** a single iris on a bench, the P-I branching tree growing around it, a Φ gauge with twilight band set beside one observer, then the whole tree labeled Omega. The status cartouche changes at each stage.
- **Stages:**
  1. Holos starts from one fact: experience exists.
  2. Physics as it stands: every branch of the one quantum state.
  3. First addition: a threshold, where a point of view forms.
  4. Second addition: Omega, the whole, read as the one experiencer.
  5. Everything else is marked: a side taken, open, or a companion idea.
- **Risk:** status drift, so that everything looks equally established. Keep the cartouches, and label Omega on the whole tree, never its crown.
- **Reuse:** P-I stop 6.

### Primitives

**L2. A Difference Within Structure** (D1, L97)
- **Shows:** an engraved coin turning on a table, heads and tails shown as two ghosted states, with a leader line to "the difference". Then the table and coin are removed and the label has nothing to point to.
- **Stages:**
  1. A coin can land two ways.
  2. The difference between those states is information.
  3. It is not a substance. Take away the coin and there is no information left over.
- **Risk:** information drawn as glowing stuff that flows or floats. Draw only the two states and a leader line.

**L3. Constraints Make Structure** (D2, L107; also fits Axiom 1 and Proposition I)
- **Shows:** a brass linkage (pantograph or four-bar). Nodes are states and rods are constraints. Moving one joint moves the others; cutting a rod frees them.
- **Stages:**
  1. States, each able to differ.
  2. A relation links two states: one constrains the other.
  3. Move one and the linked ones co-vary.
  4. Structure is a stable pattern of such relations.
- **Risk:** the hand that moves the joint reads as an agent or as observation. Use a crank driven by the mechanism itself, and keep irises off this figure.

**L4. Registration From Within** (D3, L117)
- **Shows:** a world (a lamp, a tree) sending traces inward, drawn as arrows, to three systems: a rock, a busy network of separate processes, and an integrated network whose loop gathers the traces into one internal state, marked with an iris. A small Φ gauge sits under each system.
- **Stages:**
  1. Signals from a world reach many systems.
  2. A rock takes part in the interaction, with no point of view.
  3. A busy network processes the signals without integrating them.
  4. An observer integrates them into one internal state. This is observation.
  5. The lamp and the tree are unchanged.
- **Risk:** arrows out of the observer, or the world changing when it is registered. Arrows point inbound only, and the world's lines stay fixed.
- **Reuse:** P-II stops 1 to 4.

**L5. Fundamental, Not Everywhere** (D4, second paragraph, L143)
- **Shows:** a row of specimens: a stone, a thermostat, a river, a mouse, a person. A Φ gauge with twilight band sits under each. Only the specimens past the band carry irises, and the iris widths differ between them.
- **Stages:**
  1. Holos starts from experience rather than deriving it. That is all "fundamental" means.
  2. It does not mean experience is everywhere: well below the threshold there is none.
  3. Past the threshold, the structure fixes the experience.
  4. Its form varies with structure and scales with integration.
- **Risk:** reads as a panpsychist gradient, or as a dial on the gauge causing the iris to open. Leave sub-threshold items bare (no faint iris), and draw no arrow from gauge to iris.
- **Reuse:** P-II gauge and iris.

**L6. Creation: The Branches Physics Produces** (D5, L158)
- **Shows:** one root state growing into an engraved branching tree that rotates on its axis. A second, ghosted tree labeled "other beginnings" stands to one side and is struck through.
- **Stages:**
  1. One state of the universe.
  2. Physics produces every branch from it.
  3. Read without collapse, every branch is kept.
  4. Worlds from other beginnings are not Creation. They are mere possibilities, not structure.
  5. Creation fixes what happens in every branch, not what is experienced.
- **Risk:** the ghost tree reads as part of C, and the growth reads as history. Strike the ghost tree clearly, and keep irises off this figure (they belong to D6).
- **Reuse:** P-I stops 1 and 2.

**L7. Possibility, Then Registration** (D6, L176; repeat or link at Notation, L1610)
- **Shows:** the D5 tree drawn on one engraved plate. A transparent overlay plate is laid over it that carries only iris marks, set partway along a few branches. A dimension note reads "lines beneath: unchanged". The overlay stays in place, and the caption states that the order is one of logic.
- **Stages:**
  1. C: the branches physics produces.
  2. O: registration, wherever a branch holds an observer.
  3. Registering changes nothing beneath it.
  4. R = C ⊛ O: the family of lived perspectives, one per observer per branch.
  5. The order is logical, not temporal: registering needs something to register.
- **Risk:** laying the overlay reads as an event in time, and irises at forks read as observation making branches. Put irises only partway along branches, and let the narration correct the time reading.
- **Reuse:** P-I stops 3 to 5.

**L8. Lived, Lit, Unlit, Witnessing** (D7 bullets, L200)
- **Shows:** the P-III block universe with one world line and its past light cone, engraved in the same pass as the block. Iris marks sit on the world line. Everything outside the cone is soft ink. Witnessing is denser hatching along traces (starlight, fossil record). A second small observer-free block sits beside it.
- **Stages:**
  1. Lived: inside observers, and nowhere else.
  2. Lit: everything in an observer's causal past. Starlight puts a star there.
  3. The early universe is lit, but no one lived through it.
  4. Unlit: outside every observer's causal past, and whole branches with no observer.
  5. Witnessing is graded: how much of the lit region experience is actually about.
- **Risk:** the cone drawing itself in says that lighting happens. Engrave it with the block, point arrows inbound only, and keep lit as uniform full ink.
- **Reuse:** P-III stops 3 to 6 directly.

**L9. Shared History Is Lit** (D7, last sentence of the Lit bullet, L213)
- **Shows:** a 3D tree in which each branch is a thin spacetime slab. Branch A grows an observer, whose past cone runs back down through the trunk. Branch B, which splits from the same trunk, has no observer. The trunk below the split is full ink; branch B above the split is soft ink.
- **Stages:**
  1. A stretch of history shared by two branches.
  2. One branch later holds an observer.
  3. Its causal past reaches back into the shared trunk, so the trunk is lit.
  4. The sibling branch, above the split, holds no observer and stays unlit.
- **Risk:** reads as the observer lighting the trunk backward in time. The trunk is inked in the same pass, and the caption says lit is a fact about arrangement, not an event.
- **Reuse:** a hybrid of P-I and P-III.

**L10. One Lights, Many Witness** (D7 Witnessing bullet and the tenseless paragraph, L223-233)
- **Shows:** two observers' world lines with overlapping past cones in one block. The overlap is lit once (uniform ink), while witnessing hatching differs per observer inside it. Each observer's future cone is left soft.
- **Stages:**
  1. Two observers, two causal pasts.
  2. Where they overlap, the region is lit, no more and no less.
  3. Each witnesses it differently, in amount and detail.
  4. The relation points one way: an observer lights its past, not its future.
- **Risk:** double ink in the overlap would make lit look graded. Keep the lit ink uniform and put all the variation in the witnessing hatching.
- **Reuse:** P-III.

### Axioms

**L11. The Core and the Two Versions** (`#logic-axioms` intro, L254)
- **Shows:** an engraved stone arch. Four voussoirs (Axioms 1, 3, 4, 5) make the core. Axiom 2 is a keystone slot with two interchangeable keystones on a rack: "without collapse (defended)" and "with collapse (declared in advance)". The arch stands with either.
- **Stages:**
  1. Five axioms.
  2. Two are additions to physics: 3 and 5.
  3. The core, Axioms 1, 3, 4 and 5, holds on any reading of quantum physics.
  4. Axiom 2 is the one side taken on quantum physics itself.
  5. It divides Holos into two versions that share the core.
- **Risk:** a fork shape would echo quantum branching, and an arch implies Axiom 2 bears load in both versions. Label the keystones as versions, not branches, and use a cartouche for "defended".

**L12. A Description Made of Relations** (Axiom 1 commentary, L275)
- **Shows:** a network with every link labeled, and a ledger listing each relation. An observer region inside the network carries an iris. A leader from the iris to the ledger finds no line to point to: "is lived" is not an entry.
- **Stages:**
  1. Physics describes relations: how states constrain one another.
  2. The ledger is complete. Every relation is in it.
  3. That a state is lived is not a relation among states.
  4. So physics states experience only from outside.
- **Risk:** reads as experience being a hidden extra thing (dualism). Caption: "a limit of the description, not a missing part of the world."

**L13. Nothing Is Erased** (Axiom 2, L290)
- **Shows:** a letter burning on a plate, with smoke, heat and light carried outward as arrows into the room's air molecules. The letter's detail is scrambled but still present. A reversal arrow shows the dynamics running back in principle. Then a spin splits into two branches, each holding its outcome.
- **Stages:**
  1. Burn a letter and its words seem gone.
  2. The detail spreads into smoke, light and heat. It is scrambled, not destroyed.
  3. In principle the state keeps enough to reconstruct its past.
  4. With no collapse, every outcome remains, each in its own branch.
  5. Observation selects nothing and erases nothing.
- **Risk:** implying reconstruction is practical, or that observation does the erasing. Label "in principle", and keep observers out of the scene.

**L14. The Threshold at a Local Maximum** (Axiom 3, L312)
- **Shows:** a relief landscape of integration over nested candidate systems (a hemisphere, a brain, a brain plus a partner), with a hatched twilight plane cutting through it. One peak rises past the plane and carries an iris. A gauge sits alongside.
- **Stages:**
  1. Integration: how far a system's parts act as one whole.
  2. Well below the threshold, no experience at all.
  3. A narrow twilight, with no exact fact.
  4. Past it, at a local maximum, an observer.
  5. Not a force or a field: a structural fact about where observers occur.
- **Risk:** the plane read as a sharp line, or the landscape read as a physical force field. Draw the twilight as a hatched slab with thickness, and give the landscape no arrows.
- **Reuse:** P-II gauge; see also L31.

**L15. One Curve, Two Sides** (Axiom 4, L339, and `#kind-of-view`, L396)
- **Shows:** a single engraved curved strip that the camera circles. The convex side is labeled OUTSIDE: PHYSICAL ACTIVITY, the concave side INSIDE: EXPERIENCE. Then a perfect copy of the strip is made, and both sides come with it.
- **Stages:**
  1. One curve, two true descriptions.
  2. Seen from outside: physical activity.
  3. Lived from inside: experience.
  4. Neither side comes first, and neither occurs without the other.
  5. Copy the outside exactly and the inside comes with it.
- **Risk:** two sides read as two things layered together. One strip, one line weight, and no gap between the faces.
- **Reuse:** P-II stop 7.

**L16. The Floor Plan and the House** (Axiom 4, third paragraph, L375)
- **Shows:** a draughtsman's floor plan that records every wall. A house rises from it wall for wall. The camera moves into a lived room with a chair, a lamp and a window. The plan's legend has no entry for "what living here is like".
- **Stages:**
  1. A floor plan records every wall.
  2. It cannot say what living there is like.
  3. Build the house exactly from the plan and it is livable all the same.
  4. The gap is in the description, not in the world.
- **Risk:** implies the house adds something the plan left out physically. The dimension line reads "identical to the plan".

**L17. Three Kinds of View** (`#kind-of-view`, L396-409)
- **Shows:** three specimens side by side.
  - Dualism: two separate objects, body and ghost, linked by a question mark.
  - Physicalism: the curve with an arrow from outside to inside, "grounded in", like a table's solidity in its atoms.

  1. Dualism: two kinds of thing.
  2. Physicalism: one thing, the inside grounded in the outside.
  3. Holos agrees on the facts: copy the outside and you copy the inside.
  4. It differs on direction: neither side is grounded in the other.
  5. And on scope: an inside only past the threshold.
- **Risk:** caricaturing the rival views. Draw each specimen with equal care, and add a leader noting that some file Holos under physicalism.

**L18. Where the One Subject Wakes** (Axiom 5, L417)

### Foundational Propositions

**L19. The Structure Survives the Story** (Proposition I, L483)
- **Shows:** a 19th-century wave-optics apparatus. An engraved ether mechanism (gears, an elastic medium) fades between theories, while the lattice of wave equations stays drawn throughout, relabeled from "ether" to "field".
- **Stages:**
  1. A theory says what exists and how it relates.
  2. Later, what it says exists is replaced.
  3. The relational structure is kept.
  4. What theories track are stable patterns of relation.
- **Risk:** Fresnel-to-Maxwell is the standard structural-realism example but is not in the text. Mark it as an illustration, or substitute a neutral example (a chess knight defined by its moves, carved in wood then ivory).

**L20. Two Sunsets, the Same Light** (Proposition II, L522)
- **Shows:** two identical engraved planets side by side, with sunsets and ray paths line for line the same. One holds an observer (iris) on its shore. A dimension line reads IDENTICAL.
- **Stages:**
  1. A sunset no one ever sees.
  2. The same sunset, the same light, watched by someone.
  3. Physics specifies consistency. Every line is the same.
  4. The difference Holos marks: the second is lived.
- **Risk:** the lived planet brighter or more detailed would suggest observation changes it. Same ink everywhere except the iris; the planet's lit cone is inked in the same pass.
- **Reuse:** P-VIII stops 1 and 2.

**L21. No Shared Now** (Proposition III, fourth paragraph, L561)
- **Shows:** two observers in relative motion in a spacetime diagram, their "now" slices tilted against each other while the 45° light line stays fixed. The camera pulls back to the whole block.
- **Stages:**
  1. Light's speed is the same for every observer.
  2. Observers moving differently slice "now" differently.
  3. Relativity picks out no privileged present.
  4. That motivates the block universe. It does not prove it.
  5. The block is what every perspective shares.
- **Risk:** stating the block as proven. Stage 4 carries the hedge.
- **Reuse:** P-III stops 1 and 2.

**L22. Consistent Like a Finished Puzzle** (Proposition III, L537-546)
- **Shows:** the block universe engraved as a completed 3D jigsaw, with every piece fitting its neighbors across time. An observer's world line runs through it and nothing about the fit changes.
- **Stages:**
  1. Take all of space and time as one whole.
  2. Consistency is a property of whole histories.
  3. Every part fits every other part, like a solved puzzle.
  4. Observation adds no constraint. It registers what already fits.
  5. No backward causation, no signaling.
- **Risk:** pieces snapping into place over time reads as enforcement or as an assembler. Draw the puzzle complete from the start; the camera only moves.

**L23. Why Records Agree** (Proposition III, fifth paragraph, L573)
- **Shows:** a lab bench with a pointer. Scattered light and air molecules copy its position many times over, drawn as arrows fanning out. Two observers at different spots each receive copies, and their notebooks match. A small inset shows a sealed-lab Wigner's friend setup.
- **Stages:**
  1. An ordinary record: a pointer's position.
  2. The environment copies it many times over.
  3. Two observers compare notes. The same signals reached both, so they agree.
  4. Physics secures this. The monist reading plays no role.
  5. Only a perfectly sealed-off observer makes agreement subtle (Check C).
- **Risk:** implying Omega or a hidden link enforces agreement. No line between the observers; only inbound arrows from the pointer.

**L27. Not a Path Between Places** (Proposition IV, third paragraph, L645)

- **Stages:**

  2. Sudden: the way in and the way out do not match.
  3. Continuous: slowing and growing fluctuations near the boundary.
  4. Either kind: a twilight that narrows as systems grow.
- **Risk:** implies Holos has chosen one kind. Keep the two panels symmetrical, labeled "either".

**L65. Knock and Listen: PCI** (`#path-to-threshold`, L1785-1799)
- **Shows:** a head with a magnetic coil delivering a brief pulse and an EEG cap. The echo spreads across cortex as a complex, differentiated wave in waking and dreaming, and as a local or uniform echo in deep sleep and anesthesia. A number line of PCI values carries one empirical cutoff.
- **Stages:**
  1. Give the brain a brief magnetic pulse.
  2. Record the echo with scalp electrodes.
  3. Awake: a rich, varied echo. Under anesthesia: a simple one.
  4. One cutoff, found by experiment, sorted conscious from unconscious states.
  5. No one measured presence directly: this is how Φ_c could be located.
- **Risk:** equating PCI with Φ or with presence, or treating the pulse as causing consciousness. Label it "PCI, a stand-in measure".

**L66. Joined Versus Varied** (`#path-to-threshold`, criticality paragraph, L1802-1852)
- **Shows:** a 2D engraved chart. The horizontal axis is integration, with the twilight band; the vertical axis is differentiation (variety). Waking cortex sits near the peak of variety. A seizure is far right and low: joined, little variety (a stadium chanting one word, drawn as a small inset crowd). Anesthesia sits low, psychedelics higher. A small avalanche inset shows cascades of all sizes on a log-log line.
- **Stages:**
  1. Waking cortex runs near a tipping point between order and chaos.
  2. Anesthesia and seizures move it away; psychedelics move it closer.
  3. Integration asks whether the parts are joined. That is where the threshold lies.
  4. Differentiation asks how varied the states are, and variety peaks near criticality.
  5. A seizure is joined but not varied, like a stadium chanting one word.
  6. These results show where observers operate, not that the crossing is a transition.
- **Risk:** conflating "critical point" (the operating point) with the threshold crossing, which the glossary forbids. Keep the two axes separate, and stage 6 makes the limit explicit.
- **Reuse:** P-II stop 6.

**L67. Going Under, Coming Back** (`#path-to-threshold`, neural inertia paragraph, L1855-1906)
- **Shows:** an anesthetic concentration axis with a responsiveness curve. Going down (loss) and coming up (return) take different paths: a hysteresis loop labeled "animal studies". Then a fly strain where a single mutation collapses the loop, and human panels: propofol with no loop, sevoflurane with some, and an EEG-only loop in 393 patients. The final panel is a steep, rounded curve labeled "the twilight".
- **Stages:**
  1. In animals, consciousness is lost and regained at different doses.
  2. A lag like that is the mark of an abrupt switch, like water freezing.
  3. But in flies, sleep genes control the lag, and single mutations erase it.
  4. In humans the evidence is mixed: the lag depends on the drug and shows in the EEG.
  5. In a finite brain, either kind of transition appears as a steep, rounded curve.
- **Risk:** reading the lag as proof of the transition hypothesis. Stages 3 and 4 carry the caveats; cartouche: MIXED EVIDENCE.

**L68. Keep the Measures That Agree** (`#path-to-threshold`, convergence paragraph, L1909-1926)
- **Shows:** a row of candidate measures as engraved instruments, each ranking the anchor cases (a waking adult, a sleeping adult, a deeply anesthetized adult). Instruments whose ranking disagrees are set aside. The survivors form a small labeled equivalence class.
- **Stages:**
  1. Run each candidate measure across the same systems.
  2. Anchor cases: states whose status is not in doubt.
  3. Keep the measures that rank the anchors the same way.
  4. Stronger: derive the measure from what any measure of one perspective must satisfy.
  5. Even a small family that always agrees removes most of the arbitrariness.
- **Risk:** low.

**L69. The Narrow Band That Fits Everything** (`#path-to-threshold`, "Solve them jointly", L1929-1941)
- **Shows:** four translucent engraved constraint bands in a measure-by-threshold space: anesthesia boundary, development, split brain, minimal neural systems. They intersect in a small region. Then data is split into a calibration set, which locates the region, and a sealed envelope of held-out states, which tests it.
- **Stages:**
  1. Each anchor case constrains the measure and the threshold together.
  2. Anesthesia, development, split brains, minimal neural systems.
  3. Coupled constraints leave only a narrow band.
  4. Calibration states locate the threshold.
  5. Held-out states, named in advance, test it. Testing on the calibration set would only grade the answer key.
- **Risk:** implies the band is already found. Draw it as hypothetical, with dashed outlines.

**L70. Report as Anchor, and Two Ways to Lose** (`#path-to-threshold`, last paragraph, L1972-1991)
- **Shows:** concentric rings of certainty around a human who reports, fading outward to animals, organoids and machines. An inset: a patient who cannot respond, but whose scan shows them following instructions (about 1 in 4). Then two failure plates: integration climbing smoothly with no transition (claim 3 falls, core intact, wide twilight), and experience not tracking integration at all (Test A lost, core falls).
- **Stages:**
  1. The anchor is report, the one place inside access exists.
  2. Certainty weakens as the cases grow alien.
  3. Report can miss consciousness: about one in four unresponsive patients follow instructions on scans.
  4. Failure one: no transition. Claim 3 falls, and the twilight is a slow dawn.
  5. Failure two: experience does not track integration. Test A is lost, and the core with it.
- **Risk:** the rings could read as experience fading with distance from humans. Label them "our certainty", not "their experience".
